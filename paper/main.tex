Displacement-based invariance measurement on a frozen backbone is
established: \citet{ericsson2022why} compute a whitened Mahalanobis
displacement over eighteen transforms, which is the same quantity we
denote $m_{\mathrm{total}}$, and the alignment term of
\citet{wang2020understanding} is its unwhitened form. Two recent preprints measure
invariance without probes and are the closest prior work. SEIS
\citep{lin2026seis} separates invariance from equivariance by applying
CCA to SVD-denoised activation subspaces, reports that a random baseline
yields near-zero scores, and states the motivating gap as the failure of
existing metrics ``to distinguish between information loss and
re-encoding''. That distinction is theirs and we adopt it rather than
claim it. SEIS measures alignment between subspaces under post-hoc image
transformations, defines no absolute threshold, and runs no probes.
EditCLEVR \citep{karnam2026editclevr} builds paired CLEVR scenes in which
one attribute of one object changes with the layout held fixed, and
reports probe-free localization and drift norms alongside probe-decoded
semantic metrics. Its scene set is not fully factorial, it uses one
context per edit, and it reports no untrained-encoder baseline and no
calibrated threshold; its own limitations name calibration baselines and
factor-structured readouts as next steps. Our design differs on those
points: a complete factorial grid of independently sampled factors
supplies an expectation over contexts for a fixed factor change,
whitening makes the statistic approximately coordinate-stable, and a
random-encoder null supplies a calibrated absolute threshold with a
declared band in which the certificate does not decide.
